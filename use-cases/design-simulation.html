Design Simulation

Test an idea before you build it: load, vibration, heat, impact and wind, on the part itself.

Use it freeHow it works
The problem

Why this is hard today

To know whether an idea will hold, shake, overheat or cut through the air, you export the model, rebuild it in a simulation program, set it up and wait. For a first idea nobody does it, so weak designs get all the way to the drawing before anyone finds out.

How Ensemble helps

From scattered files to one workspace.

01

Choose the material and click the faces

Pick the material, click the faces that are held and the faces that are pushed (or the one that hits the wall, or the side the wind comes from) and set the number: newtons, speed, temperature.

02

Run it on the part

Ensemble turns the part into a grid of small cubes and solves it right in the app. A few seconds to a couple of minutes, depending on the quality you choose.

03

See it on the real part

The result is painted on the part and the part is drawn deformed. Vibration modes move, the impact plays and stays dented, and the air flows around the part in the wind tunnel.

What you get

Built into the workspace

These are quick, indicative simulations to compare ideas. They are not certified analysis: the wind runs at a lower Reynolds number than the real one, and the impact fills the part as solid material. For a final decision, use a dedicated engineering tool.

  • Static load and own weight: stress painted on the part, the biggest displacement and a safety factor with a plain verdict
  • Vibration: natural frequencies and mode shapes, animated
  • Heat: temperature spread by conduction, with cold and hot faces and optional air cooling
  • Impact: throw the part against a rigid wall at a speed you choose; it dents, bends or breaks, with the peak force, the deceleration in g and the permanent shape
  • Wind tunnel: drag and lift in newtons, Cd and Cl, the pressure on every face, and animated air particles that show where the flow speeds up and where the wake forms
  • Air or water, in any of the six directions
  • Compare variants of a design: change one thing and run it again
