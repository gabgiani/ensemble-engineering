Electrical Documentation

The documents an electrical project is handed over with, made from the schematic you already drew.

Use it freeHow it works
The problem

Why this is hard today

A schematic is only part of what a customer or an installer expects. The connection list, the cable list, the terminal plan, the PLC I/O list and the cover with its revisions are usually retyped by hand, and they drift away from the drawing the first time something changes.

How Ensemble helps

From scattered files to one workspace.

01

Draw it once

Draw or generate the schematic, with its real symbols and terminals. Write the length and the section of the wires that matter, and the drawing number, project, author and company in the document data.

02

Make the document

One button makes an editable A3 document next to the schematic, with its title block, a cover with the data the standards ask for and what is still missing, the circuit diagram and each list. You can make several, choose which lists go in, export a PDF, or split it into pages to present.

03

Keep them current

Change the schematic and update the document: it fills again from the schematic. Add a revision with its letter and what changed, and the title block follows. Each document stays linked to its schematic by a dotted line you can cut.

What you get

Built into the workspace

These are generated from the schematic and are only as complete as the schematic: a cable with no length or section is listed without them. Panel layout, 3D cabinet design and test reports are not part of Ensemble, which is for ideas and their communication, not for detailed CAD.

  • Connection list: every wire from terminal to terminal, with net, section and length
  • Cable list with the current each cable carries, the voltage drop and the smallest section that carries it
  • Terminal plan: each terminal with what is wired to either side
  • PLC I/O list: each channel with the device wired to it
  • Cross-references: each coil with the contacts it operates, normally open or closed, with their terminal marks
  • Label list for devices and terminals, and device data with rating, design current and heat loss
  • Cover with the data IEC 60204-1 asks for, a list of what is still missing, a revisions table and an ISO 7200 title block on every sheet, exported as a PDF
  • Standards behind them: IEC 61082, IEC 81346, IEC 60617, IEC 60204-1, IEC 61439 and IEC 61131
