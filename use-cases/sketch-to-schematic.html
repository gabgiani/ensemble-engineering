Sketch to Schematic

Draw a circuit by hand and get a clean schematic with real parts, a bill of materials and a simulation.

Use it freeHow it works
The problem

Why this is hard today

Circuits start as a sketch on paper or a tablet. Redrawing them in an electrical CAD program takes an afternoon, picking the right symbol for every device takes another, and by then the sketch and the schematic no longer match.

How Ensemble helps

From scattered files to one workspace.

01

Draw it by hand

Sketch the circuit with the pen on the canvas, an Apple Pencil on iPad. Rough is fine: a lamp, a switch, a fuse, a battery and the wires between them. Right-click the sketch and choose the electronic or the industrial electrical schematic.

02

The AI builds the schematic

The AI reads the strokes and places each device with a real symbol from the library, wired terminal to terminal. For industrial diagrams it looks the devices up in the real IEC symbol library and in the ETIM classification instead of inventing them. The schematic appears next to your sketch, joined to it by a connector you can break.

03

Refine it, then use it

Chat with the AI to change the schematic (“add a second lamp with its own fuse”), or edit it by hand in the single-panel editor. Make the bill of materials with one line, and open the Simulate tab to check the voltages and the power.

What you get

Built into the workspace

The AI needs a service connected in the settings, and the industrial and electronic symbol libraries are downloaded once from the Components tab. The sketch has to be readable: the AI reads devices and connections, it does not guess what is not drawn. The simulation is quick and indicative, not a certified calculation.

  • Electronic and industrial electrical schematics, each with its own real symbol library
  • Real symbols and terminals: KiCad for electronics, IEC/EN 60617 for industrial, never a made-up shape
  • The generated schematic stays linked to its sketch, with a connector you can break
  • Change it by chatting with the AI, or edit it by hand with Properties, Components and Simulate tabs
  • A bill of materials table in one line, with the footprint of each electronic part
  • Industrial parts carry their ETIM class and technical data, and terminals are marked as IEC 60947 asks (1/L1, 3/L2, A1, A2, 13-14…)
  • Simulate the voltage drop and the power of the circuit, and open a contact to see what happens
