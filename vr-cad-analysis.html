VR · Meta Quest

CAD analysis in VR.

Put on the headset and walk around your real CAD model, with the Gantt and the Kanban in the same space. Pin, measure and cut the part together with your team, in one shared workspace.

Get the Quest appRequest web access

Built for Meta Quest (tested on Quest 2). Sideloaded app, for now.

One space, three things

The part, the analysis and the plan.

01

See the real CAD

STEP and SolidWorks models open as true 3D meshes at human scale. Grab a part with the side grip, turn it with your wrist, scale it with the stick, and walk around it.

02

Analyse it in 3D

Pin comments and labels on the surface, measure distances and angles, cut sections along X, Y or Z, show the cut part as a mesh, hide parts of an assembly, and see the analysis highlights on the part.

03

The plan beside it

Every object of the workspace floats as a card around you: the Gantt and the Kanban sit next to the part, and a pin takes you from a task to the exact spot on the model.

Collaborative

Everyone looks at the same workspace.

VR is one more way into the shared workspace, not a separate copy. What your colleagues change on the canvas shows up on the floating part, and what you add in VR shows up on their screens.

  • Turn, colour, section cut, mode and analysis highlight follow in real time on parts set to live
  • Pins, labels and measurements are shared with the whole workspace
  • Live Showcase: the presenter turns the part in the air and the viewers, in VR or on a screen, see it and can walk around it (read-only)
  • Works with DAFONET or your own company server, with invitation-only access
Made for the headset

The canvas, around you.

A canvas you can shape

Move it, bring it closer or farther, widen it and curve it around you with the controllers. One click puts it back in front of you.

Real keyboard, real clicks

A floating keyboard with symbols and @, and click with the trigger or with A / X, which stays steady on small buttons.

A guide inside the app

A built-in controls guide opens from the “?” button, so nobody has to remember the buttons.

On a screen, too

The same workspace, in and out of the headset.

These are screens from the app. In VR the same objects float around you, so the person in the headset and the person at the desk work on the same thing.
